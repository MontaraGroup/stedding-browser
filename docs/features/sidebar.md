# Feature: Sidebar density, text size and the collapsed rail

Status: **Y1–Y14, Y16–Y27 built** (Y22–Y24 since beta 14, Y25 in patch 0068; Y9, Y10, Y12, Y13 with their tests and Y26 on 2026-10-18; Y27's tests pass on the Mac since 2026-10-26); **Y15 partial** (not yet heard with a screen reader, BACKLOG S-129); **Y28 built** 2026-10-20; **Y30–Y33 built** 2026-10-22; **Y29 built** 2026-10-20 on the Mac (its Windows test not yet run); **Y34–Y41, Y43 built** (written 2026-10-25, the sidebar on the right, BACKLOG S-96, released in 0.3.0 beta 16), their tests passing on the Mac (2026-10-26), not yet seen on a build; **Y42 partial** (the caption buttons on Windows): its unit test passes on the Mac (2026-10-26), its Windows browser test did not run there; **Y44 built** 2026-10-25, released in 0.3.0 beta 17; **Y45 built** 2026-10-26, not yet released.
Owner docs: `docs/PRODUCT.md#ui-spec` (the measured Arc match), `docs/PRODUCT.md` §1. Patches: 0031, 0037 (Y6–Y7), 0059 (Y18–Y21), 0060 (Y22–Y24), 0063 (Y14).

The sidebar's rows are Arc's: 44 DIP with an 18 DIP favicon, measured in
`docs/PRODUCT.md#ui-spec`. Two browser-wide settings let a user trade that for more rows on the
screen, or a larger title: a density preset and one text-size step. Comfortable is the
measured match and stays exactly as it is.

## Notes

- The collapsed rail (Y6, Y7) is laid out by `VerticalTabStripRegionView`
  (an interior margin of the card gutter while collapsed) and
  `VerticalTabStripTopContainer` (the caption clearance, fed the traffic
  lights' height by `SteddingBrowserViewLayout`); `tooling/dev test sidebar`
  runs the top container's tests.
- The preferences are local state (browser-wide): a window's rows should not differ
  by profile, and the cache is process-wide, read from every window's layout.
- The cache is filled at startup and on every change of either preference by a
  `SidebarDensity` observer on local state; the change walks the browser list and
  invalidates each window's layout, which re-reads the constants.
- The sidebar on the right (Y34–Y43) is a flag in the layout, not a mirrored
  view. `VerticalTabStripRegionView::IsAtTrailingEdge` says the strip takes
  the window's trailing edge (the choice of side against the language's
  reading direction), and the browser view's layout places the strip, the
  page's inset and the clip for its animations by it, in six marked hunks of
  `browser_view_tabbed_layout_impl.cc`; `SteddingBrowserViewLayout` gives the
  strip's top row the exclusion over its column and the address row the
  traffic lights. Mirroring the browser view (`View::SetMirrored`) would have
  left the children's logical bounds as they were, so the page's native
  surface and the layers would not have followed a live switch, and every
  `base::i18n::IsRTL` that means the screen would have read wrong. Code that
  works on screen -- the handle's reach, the reveal edge, the drop arrow, the
  keyboard resize, the hover card's side, Windows' caption colours -- asks
  `IsOnRight`. The uses of `IsRTL` that mean reading direction stay: the
  Space switcher's slide, the swipe between Spaces, the down arrow over a
  pinned row, the rows themselves.
- Not mirrored: the window ground's diagonal gradient and the shadow of the
  rail expanded on hover, both cosmetic; macOS's glass frame, off in
  Stedding's builds (`features::kGlassFrame`), keeps its glass on the left.
